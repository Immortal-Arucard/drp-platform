Democratic-Republican Party

DRP Platform

Thirty-two proposals, as the DRP Compass states them. Each section quotes the proposal, then gives our explanation for it, and the objections we have answered. Nothing here is taken from questions we left open. The liberal or conservative mark is the side the Compass gives to more agreement.

Of the thirty-two positions, seventeen are liberal and fifteen are conservative. The Democratic-Republican Party is a radical centrist party. Here, “liberal” means on the side of liberty, the word’s true and historical meaning, which we count as right-wing, not the modern left. “Conservative” is the other side, which we count as left-wing.

1. Taxes

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

Taxes are evil and unnecessary.

We call taxes evil, and we call them unnecessary.

We call taxation theft under threat of force. In our view, government has no special permission to take money that way. It should pay for roads, courts, police, and the military the way anyone else pays bills: by earning. We do not prescribe the method, only the limit. Do not assault or rob people to get the money.

Ideally, government would take care of people’s needs and desires by selling goods and services that citizens already demand.

Frequently raised objections

Call it inefficient, not evil

We call it evil because it is theft under threat of force, not merely a clumsy way to raise money.

Who pays for roads, courts, police, and the military?

Government can earn the money without coercion. That is our answer for what replaces taxes as well.

Does the manner of earning matter?

We compare it to rent. We do not care how the money was earned, so long as no one was assaulted or robbed to get it.

2. Inflation/deflation

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

Inflation is bad, and abundance- driven deflation is good.

We call inflation bad, and we call deflation good when that deflation comes from abundance.

We treat credit as inherently valuable. Money becoming more valuable makes credit more precious, not less. We reject the idea that abundance-driven deflation must spiral through debt. On a fixed mortgage, the dollar payments and the interest stay what was agreed. We also say goods and services have inherent value, so demand can be pushed down only so far. With enough abundance, lenders can still profit at negative interest rates.

Frequently raised objections

Deflation makes debts unpayable

Credit stays valuable, and a famous crash was an overleveraged house of cards, not abundance. The agreed dollar payments do not rise, and we reject treating a higher real burden as decisive when wages do not fall as far as prices.

People will wait to buy if tomorrow is cheaper

Goods and services have inherent value, so demand can be suppressed only to a point, even for luxuries. Lower prices then show what things are really worth.

Lenders cannot survive falling prices

With enough abundance-driven deflation, lending can profit even at negative interest rates.

3. Money not hegemonic value

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as conservative.

Money should not be the hegemonic unit of value in society.

We hold that money should not be the hegemonic unit of value in society.

We do not mean that money must stop being widely used, or even that it cannot sit on top. We mean it must not be the only yardstick, and that too few things count as money. Exchanges become unfair when objective value in things other than money is barely recognized.

We want more kinds of money, provided fraud can be investigated. Other value systems should be formalized at least enough to judge fairness after a transaction, and at a certain level they must be financially unregulated. Barter has to remain valid. That does not make it legal to barter illegal goods, such as slaves.

Frequently raised objections

If money is on top, it is already hegemonic

That is a strawman. The top spot is not the same thing as hegemony, and we also object that too few things are allowed to count as money.

4. Free speech

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

Free speech must be absolute, short of explicitly wishing for or celebrating death or bodily harm upon a person or a people.

We hold that free speech must be absolute, short of explicitly wishing for or celebrating death or bodily harm upon a person or a people.

We also describe the forbidden speech as wishing for, calling for, or celebrating murder or other permanent physical injury. The two wordings do not necessarily contradict, and either is likely to be enough.

The rule applies in state, corporate, private, and other settings. Dangerous speech is to be punished. Every other kind of speech is to be defended. Punishment itself must stay moral.

Frequently raised objections

Employers, platforms, and crowds may still punish speech

An employer, a platform, a mob, and a government each have a right and a responsibility to censor and punish speech that meets our definition. Those same bodies punishing any other speech are guilty of high villainy.

Punish bad speech by any means that works

No. We want immoral speech punished only by moral means.

5. Abortion & contraception

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as conservative.

Abortion, including contraceptives and all forms of pregnancy prevention or negation, should be prohibited, except to prevent the death of the mother.

We prohibit abortion, including contraceptives and every form of pregnancy prevention or negation, except to prevent the death of the mother.

We treat eggs, sperm, and fetuses as living persons, and we rank human life first. We oppose contraception both because some methods act after conception and because prevention as a whole is wrong. Natural losses are blameless. Deliberate prevention is not, and so is negligent prevention: you can be wrong without intending the harm.

We call abortion murder. The exception is likely death, not mere risk. The parents decide. If they disagree, the doctors use their best judgment. We call abstinence immoral, but we would leave it legal, because outlawing it would require forcing sex.

Frequently raised objections

Any risk to the mother is enough

No. Likely death makes it the parents’ decision. If the parents do not agree, the doctors use their best judgment. Risk is inherent to life.

Only intentional harm counts

You can also be wrong in a negligent way. The law would punish the act if she knew the cost when she did it, and it would also look at anyone who reasonably should have prevented it.

6. Immigration

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

We should enable unlimited legal immigration by controlling immigrants in a strong, authoritarian way.

We call for unlimited legal immigration, with immigrants controlled in a strong, authoritarian way.

We mean immigrants would be second-class citizens under total surveillance and policing, largely told how to live good lives. It is voluntary. No one is forced to come. The measures are for their protection and for the existing fabric of society. They may leave whenever they want.

Citizenship comes after a person proves themselves over a sufficient period, if they want it. A citizen is no longer second class. Children born here would pass through the same probation, and we expect they would find it easier. Birthright citizenship, in our view, could come with that probation. Everyone has a right to watch the watchers.

Frequently raised objections

The controls are forced on people

No one is forced to immigrate. The terms are voluntary, for the immigrants’ protection and for the society they enter. They can leave whenever they want.

They remain second class forever

Once a person proves themselves over a sufficient time, they should have citizenship if they want it, and then they are no longer second class.

Children born here should skip the rules

They go through the same probation, which we expect will be easier for them. Birthright citizenship could still come with probation.

Who watches that police power?

Everyone has a right to watch the watchers. The mechanism has not been described.

7. American Monarchy & voting

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

We should adopt an American Monarchy, and greatly restrict the right to vote and the democratic mechanisms of our society.

We call for an American monarchy, and for a great restriction of the vote and of democratic mechanisms.

Monarchy is superior because people fear the truth and to some degree cannot handle it, while a king can pursue truth more courageously. The people’s power to select a new king cannot, in our view, be fully suppressed, and the people are generally a larger threat to the king than the king is to them, so the system sustains and cleans itself in a way democracy does not. Tragic as a king’s personal fate may be, monarchy makes a superior society.

The form we name is an elective monarchy through a witenagemot, a basileia, not a president and a senate. Crowning a king takes a unanimous decision, and so does deposing one. If no king is agreed, the best available man is appointed Lord Protector and Highest Steward of the Realm by a simple majority. We call the arrangement a golden triad of light. The search for new prospects never stops. Civil conflict need not be to the death, and replacing the present government should happen without getting anyone killed.

People would still vote on what the executives allow, at local, state, and federal level. They would not have a formal power to depose, only the power to make their voices heard. Only the witenagemot can formally depose. As we recall it, the council has about twenty-seven members, nine from each of three categories, sitting for life, expelled only by unanimous decision of the other members and the king or steward. An expelled member has a sacred right to a speech, public or private, of any length, recorded for posterity. A Lord Protector does not veto the council. The Lord Protector has one vote.

Frequently raised objections

Monarchies decline and tear themselves apart

Take a long-lived empire: a thousand years is stable, and a ruler who lasted that long and was then deposed deserved it.

Every monarchy is only as good as its worst heir

Our design is election for fitness, not birth. Crowning and deposing both take unanimity. A Lord Protector fills a gap. The search for prospects is constant. Conflict over the crown need not be to the death.

A bad king becomes a tyrant

Our answer is the council’s unanimous power to depose, and our claim that the system cleans itself because the people are the greater threat to the king.

8. Climate & environment

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as conservative.

The climate and the environment should be protected using a reasonable level of coercion.

We hold that the climate and the environment should be protected using a reasonable level of coercion.

Nothing is completely off the table. What keeps coercion reasonable is using the minimum necessary amount. A tool our government otherwise prohibits, a tax for example, is allowed only with an objective moral justification that it is effective and the least coercive effective option. Without that, the government has committed a violent crime.

We think warming is real and might justify some marginal coercion. We do not treat it as a reason to long for colder weather. We agree that the serious force of the policy is for direct pollution, such as sewage in rivers and smog.

Frequently raised objections

Emissions rules price families out of cars

We agree that if a lighter measure would work, the heavier rule fails the minimum-necessary test, and the government that imposed it is in the wrong.

Warming is not real, or not serious

We think it is real and might justify some marginal coercion. We also say we are supposed to be deep in an ice age, so we do not badly pine for colder weather. We agree the picture is real but not apocalyptic, with the real teeth reserved for direct pollution.

Must Congress act, or an agency?

We do not care who does the right thing by the right standard, only that someone does, and we hope it is done in time.

Climate policy is a back door to socialism

We agree that is an objective. Our answer is that those who want the coercion should want us to be the ones doing it.

9. Deistic God

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

We believe that science and logic have proven the existence of a deistic God.

We say science and logic have proved a deistic God.

Our proof runs in this order. Energy defines consciousness. Mass and energy are convertible. Boundaries are inherently imperfect and to a great degree imaginary. Therefore the universe is one living entity, and we are a very small part of it. If those steps hold, we can think of no better definition of God than the universe. We also say nothing lacks consciousness, because everything is energy.

We do not claim a separate creator. We claim the universe is eternal and call it God. We keep the word deistic, and we reject the idea that deism forbids intervention or requires a split between God and the world. Miracles and what we call magic are real, and God can intervene, but not as a cartoon that breaks the laws of physics. Those laws are never broken. We say energy and light are substances, corporeal and tangible.

This is our argument, not the kalam cosmological argument. We reject other names that collapse the view into a doctrine we do not accept.

Frequently raised objections

Deism means a God who does not intervene

We reject the attack. We do not grant that classical deists respected physical law more than we do. Intervention, in our view, never breaks the laws of physics, and we ask on what authority deists deny it.

Then this is a different doctrine under another name

We reject that name and do not accept the charge.

Consciousness cannot come from what lacks it

We are not saying it cannot. We say nothing lacks it, because everything is energy.

Energy is a measurement, not a substance

We say energy and light are substances, corporeal and tangible. Denying that is dualism, or a misunderstanding of the word substance. We treat the move as the same fallacy as placing God outside the laws of physics.

You cannot calculate fine-tuning from a single universe

By that standard of precision, you cannot calculate anything. No physical measurement is perfect.

If God can be eternal, why not the universe?

That is our view. We do not claim a creator. We claim the universe is eternal, and we call it God.

10. Nonlethal interventionism

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

In foreign policy, we should adopt a stance of hyper-agressive, nonlethal interventionism.

We call for a foreign policy of hyper-agressive, nonlethal interventionism. That is the spelling in the text.

We intervene because you cannot trust people to work together or to refrain from killing one another. The means we name are standard warfare, heavy research into nonlethal warfare, and extremely strong defense. We are calling for a unifying conquest of the earth. It can be done if we want it done, and we grant that interventionism bleeds you.

Favorites among nations are allowed, and they exist for a reason, but everyone is welcome on the winning side. We do not care which country carries the policy out. Doing it peacefully is how a country earns it. We agree with many criticisms of America. Both sides in a conflict are almost always terrible, and it still matters which is worse.

Frequently raised objections

People can be left to settle their own peace

We intervene because we do not trust people to work together or to stop killing one another.

It cannot be done, or it costs too much

It can be done if we choose to do it. We grant that interventionism bleeds a country, and we still call for a unifying conquest.

Playing favorites is hypocrisy

Everyone has favorites, for a reason. Everyone is still welcome on the winning side. We agree with many criticisms of our own country, and we do not care which country does what we prescribe, so long as it is done peacefully.

A leader who hurts their own country for an ally

Whether that charge is true or not, it is irrelevant to this stance.

This sorts the world into good guys and bad guys

It does, and not incorrectly. What matters is which side is worse, while remembering that both sides are almost always terrible.

Wars are sold on false pretexts

What is the relevance? We do not trust those accounts, which is why we made our own case.

11. Human inequality

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

We should challenge and debunk the anti-scientific lie that people are equal to one another.

We say we should challenge and debunk what we call the anti-scientific lie that people are equal to one another.

There is no sense in which people are equal, including ability, worth, and rights. Many people do claim literal equality, in public and in private, while behavior and law contradict the claim. We call that pandering, posturing, and hallucination.

Equality as a goal is immoral, even though that sentence is outside the written claim. Mutual dignity does not require it. Diversity and equality are zero-sum in our view. Diversity is good, and equality is bad. We reject the softer line that diversity only means something because people are unequal.

Frequently raised objections

People only mean equal worth and equal rights

They do mean it literally, and they are willing to say so clearly. Putting that aside, there is no sense in which people are equal.

Equality is an ideal, not a description

That is outside the written claim. We still call the goal immoral, and nothing about mutual dignity requires equality.

12. Tariffs

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

Tariffs for the purpose of stimulating an abundance of production are a moral good.

We call tariffs a moral good when their purpose is stimulating an abundance of production.

The tariff exists first to push overproduction and to create positive, abundance-driven deflation. It fails if it is not truly prohibitive, or if it is not enforced. If a tariff is prohibitive as intended, and overproduction and deflation relative to global demand still do not follow, that would be strong evidence that we are wrong.

We treat it as a tool for a world of rival nations. After unification, there is no need for it, because the correct fiscal policy can then be set directly. Gains from trade should not stop at a border, but national security requires that they do until unification. The state, in our account, insures the financial survival of affected essential businesses for a reasonable period and legally requires sufficient production in every sector.

Frequently raised objections

The claim cannot be falsified

We disagree. If the tariff is actually prohibitive and enforced, and overproduction and deflation relative to global demand do not follow, that is strong evidence we are wrong.

Trade gains should not stop at the border

We agree they should not. National security requires the stop until unification.

A world government makes tariffs pointless

We agree there is no need after unification. The correct fiscal policy can then be dictated.

Other countries will boycott or retaliate

Boycotts are irrelevant to the logic. They are either self-harm or a healthy correction. On a famous tariff collapse, the economy was overleveraged and had it coming, and exports are overrated.

Some goods cannot be grown here

Our example is bananas. Our answer is greenhouses.

A high-tariff country already failed

We do not know that country’s details, and a tariff fails when it is not prohibitive or not well enforced.

Small importers are ruined in the transition

The state insures the financial survival of affected essential businesses for a reasonable period, and the law requires sufficient production in every sector.

13. Prisoner freedoms

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as conservative.

Prisoners should be free to work a normal job for a normal competitive wage, purchase and use land and property normally, and spend unlimited amounts of time with any friends, family, or others, who wish to spend that time with the prisoner.

We hold that prisoners should be free to work a normal job at a normal competitive wage, to purchase and use land and property normally, and to spend unlimited time with anyone who wishes to spend it with them.

We want punishment to be psychological, not physical. Psychic misery hurts more efficiently and is humane, and corrective pain for habilitation is easy to inflict that way. Making a prisoner suffer beyond that reveals criminality in the punisher. A murderer is eternally damned for what they did. To make them suffer for reasons other than regret at their actions does a disservice to that goal.

Restitution would occur, and from the prisoner’s side it would look the way taxes look now. The prison would take the form of a city or town. A gang goes in with its boss, and crime-fighting continues as normal.

Frequently raised objections

Housing a gang with its boss lets the gang run the prison

That reading is ridiculous. Putting a gang in with its boss is not handing them the prison. Crime-fighting continues as normal.

Then prisoners are owed a minimum wage

We were offered a comparison with very low pay for disabled workers. That comparison is not really relevant.

Treatment addresses effects, not causes

Treat the effects too, and then look to our other relevant issues for the causes. We have not named which issues.

A prison that profits will jail more people, including innocents

That would not be a large problem if prison were the decent place we want. On innocents, we agree that the answer is the demand for proof beyond a reasonable doubt, and for jury trials.

14. Evidence standards & jury trials

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as conservative.

Judicial standards of evidence lower than beyond a reasonable doubt, and non-jury trials, should be abolished, both criminally and civilly.

We abolish standards of evidence below beyond a reasonable doubt, and we abolish non-jury trials, both in criminal cases and in civil ones.

We include small civil cases. A case is a case. We agree that jurors can fail, and we answer that any twelve jurors are likely to be at least as fair as an average judge, because there are a lot of bad judges.

If that is not enough, judges must meet a drastically higher standard, and the accused chooses the format. The choice is an addition, not a substitute: the higher standard for judges, and the accused’s choice between a jury and a judge whose identity is disclosed first. We have said that with the higher standard, juries could even be dispensed with.

Frequently raised objections

Small civil cases cannot bear that standard

A case is a case. We include the small ones.

Jurors are less fair than judges

We agree there is a problem with jurors, and any twelve are likely at least as fair as an average judge. There are a lot of bad judges.

Then raise the standard for judges and drop juries

We agree the standard for judges must be drastically higher. We also want the accused to choose between a jury and a disclosed judge. Both parts apply. They are not alternatives that cancel each other.

15. Police & no lethal force

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as conservative.

We must hire many more police, pay them much better, and abolish the use of lethal force in law enforcement.

We call for hiring many more police, paying them much better, and abolishing lethal force in law enforcement.

More police, because we do not deter enough crime. Better pay, because the work is extremely hard and dangerous, and it will be more dangerous still when it is nonlethal only.

Abolition is not immediate. It follows the same timeline as the regulation of lethal weapons generally: lethal force goes once nonlethal weapons are comparably effective and available. Until then, lethal weapons are overwhelmingly more effective, and we hold that there is a sacred right to use them to defend loved ones and to enforce law and order. Killing is wrong in principle, and the lack of a comparable nonlethal option is the excuse. Police are held to the same standard as anyone else.

Frequently raised objections

Abolish lethal force immediately

No. It ends on the same timeline as other lethal weapons, once nonlethal weapons are comparably effective and available.

Armed suspects and other hard cases make that impossible

Until nonlethal weapons match lethal ones, those cases fall under the right to use the more effective weapon in defense and in enforcing order.

Hold police to a stricter standard

No. The standard is the same as for anyone else. An officer who kills without need faces the same consequences as anyone in that situation.

A nonlethal weapon can still kill

That death is judged by intent, and by negligence or competence.

Why more police, and why more pay?

More police, because crime is not deterred enough. More pay, because the job is extremely hard and dangerous, and more dangerous still when lethal force is gone.

16. Humans naturally wicked

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as conservative.

Resolved: humans are naturally wicked.

We resolve that humans are naturally wicked.

We mean we are born into sin and live in it, because staying alive takes from the rest of the world: food, shelter, and a mother’s body, before any choice is possible. That is not a reason to hate yourself. It is easier to be good once you realize you are not good inherently.

Babies are not a blank start. Good and evil are about living morally or immorally. Nature is good, because life is a blessing, and nothing is neutral. Life is divine, and survival requires sin. The conscience exists to make that necessary sin more efficient, and being more efficient largely means being a better person.

Frequently raised objections

Babies are born neutral

No. From birth, a baby lives by taking: food, shelter, and the mother’s body. Nothing in the world is truly neutral.

Good and evil are only human labels

Good and evil are about living morally or immorally. Nature is good, not bad and not neutral.

If nature is good, a hunter’s killing cannot be wicked

Nature is good because the hunter, the hunted, and the human caught in that hunt are all blessed with life. Life is divine, and survival still requires sin.

A conscience proves we are good underneath

The conscience proves we need one. It exists to make necessary sin more efficient, and that efficiency largely means being a better person.

17. Globalism vs nationalism

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

Globalism is superior to nationalism.

We say globalism is superior to nationalism.

For us, that means unifying the earth under one government, by weapons and strategies designed to be nonlethal, not guaranteed never to kill. The end we name is inventing immortality and resurrection, and seeing humanity become what we call angelic superbeings over millennia. Unification is the logical way to maximize the chance of that success. We find it deeply offensive that humanity is not yet a single planetary civilization in the scale we use.

We will create a monarchy, not a democracy. The first step in each country is still to persuade a majority of its citizens. We also say we use the will of the people because we understand that appeal, not because it is our creed. Taking power by the most peaceful available path makes it hard for a more violent deposition to claim the moral high ground. We do not intend to hide our intentions.

We believe every person should be proud of their race, and we protect every race. We love diversity, and we do not want any race to go extinct. The choice is to rule or be ruled. Unification, as the approved wording says, does not require dissolving cultures. A world government has reason to protect them. No one may secede. Local governance may vary, but only to a degree.

Frequently raised objections

World unity is hubris

The warning has force. We would still rather rule with a humble soul and a loving heart than be ruled by people who lack them.

Nations must be free to leave, and to rule themselves

No one can ever secede. A diversity of local governance is tolerated only to a degree.

Who keeps the rulers honest?

We point to the keepers of the world government’s constitution, and to memory of us and of our written words, as the north star.

Saying this openly will only create resistance

We do not intend to hide. The strategy still works with our intentions and values in the open, and the exposure is how we earn trust.

Low birthrates should be solved by immigration

We call the birthrate problem a crisis, and immigration the wrong solution to it. Immigration can still be good on its own terms when done right. Birthrates are a separate issue.

Lead with protecting every race, and drop “rule or be ruled”

We refused that edit. We have to earn trust without lying, and the exposure is the point.

18. Property rights

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

Except for slavery, the right to own and control physical property shall not be infringed.

We hold that, except for slavery, the right to own and control physical property shall not be infringed.

We ground that claim in natural philosophy first, and in the Constitution sometimes, as support. Our abolition of taxes is a separate issue, and we invoke it here. Government may not force a sale unless it can show a clear and present danger that national security requires the taking.

Property can be forfeited after a conviction, if there is a real reason. Zoning and homeowners’ associations are mixed: mostly illegitimate, with real safety interests still in view. Someone who refuses to leave in breach of contract should be jailed.

Frequently raised objections

The state may take property after a conviction

Yes, if it has a real reason to do so.

Taxes and forced sales are ordinary government

We stand for abolishing taxes. A forced sale is forbidden unless there is a clear and present danger that national security requires it.

Zoning and neighborhood rules are just property law

We call them mixed. Mostly illegitimate, though real safety interests remain.

Occupants who stay without right should be tolerated

If they refuse to leave and are in breach of contract, they should be jailed.

19. Slavery condemned

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as conservative.

Slavery shall forever be illegal and condemned.

We hold that slavery shall forever be illegal and condemned.

Asked how far the condemnation goes, we said both: as a moral absolute, and so that slavery cannot return under a new name. We do not count low-wage work as slavery. We do not think the constitutional exception that allows involuntary servitude as punishment for crime should be removed. We gave no further reason for that last answer.

Officials who protect trafficking, or who ignore it, are accomplices in our view, and they should also be charged for betraying their oath. Reparations are a separate topic. We do not think they are all bad. We think they should not be paid with tax dollars.

Frequently raised objections

Is the ban only moral, or also a bar on new names for the same thing?

Both. We gave that as a single answer.

Remove the punishment exception in the anti-slavery amendment

It should not be removed. We did not give a reason in this discussion.

Low wages are slavery

We do not count them as slavery.

Officials who look away from trafficking are merely negligent

We call them accomplices, to be charged with the applicable crimes and with betraying their oath.

Reparations should be paid from taxes

Reparations are a separate topic. We do not think they are all bad. We think they should not be paid with tax dollars.

20. Socialism & Communism

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

Socialism and Communism are evil to the extent that they are funded coersively or usurp the authority of the state.

We hold that Socialism and Communism are evil to the extent that they are funded coercively, or usurp the authority of the state. The platform text spells that word coersively.

On taxing the rich for social programs, our answer is that we stand for abolishing taxes. On a socialism that rejects the state, “reject” is a slippery word: rejection can be peaceful or violent, and either can be justified or not. Cultural socialism is fine and good. Subversion of institutions is not.

If banks will not lend to a cooperative, our answer is that those people should find funding elsewhere, or they are likely not responsible enough to borrow.

Frequently raised objections

Taxing the rich for social programs is the coercion you mean

Our answer is that we stand for abolishing taxes.

A socialism that rejects the state is the whole evil

Rejection can be peaceful or violent, and either can be justified or not. Cultural socialism is fine and good. Subversion of institutions is not.

Cooperatives die if banks will not lend

Anyone a bank will not lend to should find funding elsewhere, or is likely not responsible enough to borrow. We do not call for special state help.

21. Drug legalization

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

All drugs should be legalized and regulated.

We hold that all drugs should be legalized and regulated.

We largely accept that adults decide what goes into their bodies, that the law targets harm to others, that sellers must label honestly and answer for what they sell, and that a store is better than a street dealer. There are limits. We first said a drug that kills in one dose would stay banned, then revised that: lethal drugs would be legal, and regulated as a lethal bioweapon.

We sort the rest by danger. Everyday drugs are ordinary, under a maturity test. Dangerous drugs that have safe doses need a license that is extremely hard for a casual user to get. 31. Free will vs determinism

On the DRP Compass, more agreement counts as liberal.

Logic demonstrates the existence of free will in all people.

We hold that logic demonstrates the existence of free will in all people.

Our argument runs this way. Intelligence, wisdom, morality, effort, motivation, and love itself, and hate too, would all be objectively worthless in a world without free will. But we evolved these features over millennia, at huge cost. Clearly they are not worthless. We add that “useful,” “beneficial,” “preferable,” and every synonym of them require the perspective of a consciousness to be coherent. There is no possible mechanism by which usefulness can be retained in the absence of consciousness, and consciousness is the word for the ability to make those decisions. Anyone who says free will is not needed for evolution, in our view, does not understand how evolution works.

The claim that you could not have done otherwise is an assertion with zero evidence, as unprovable as it is unfalsifiable. For the opposite claim we point to strong evidence, which is everyone’s shared intuition that it is true. We also say those who deny free will because individual identities are illusions do not understand that objects have consciousness too. Everything has it, and the difference between a rock and a brain is quantitative. Boundaries are illusory to a very significant degree, though not wholly, and so are the strands of free will. All the universe together is what God is.

On miracles we give this definition: “A miracle is a natural event, worked by will like all things, that seems magical to human understanding.” Many works of will are not miracles. What makes one a miracle is that it seems magical to humans, because “miracle” is a human word and must be understood in the context of human comprehension. Miracles are a natural phenomenon, just like real magic. We have approved Augustine (The City of God, book 21, chapter 8: a wonder happens not against nature, but against what is known of nature) and Arthur C. Clarke’s Third Law (any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic) as authorities on the subject, where they are useful. We agree that love is the greatest and highest form of magic.

Frequently raised objections

Evolution works without free will

Anyone who claims that does not understand evolution. Usefulness, benefit, and preference all require a consciousness to be coherent, and no mechanism could retain usefulness without one.

A rock has no free will

Everything has it. It is much more common for the rock to choose how it falls, but it sometimes chooses whether to fall at all. When the margin between falling and not falling is smaller than the capacity with which the rock can exert its free will physically, the choice is the rock’s. That is relatively rare, but very meaningful. We call it the pebble in the stream: a pebble chooses, to some degree, its path and its resting spot as it bounces and settles in the riverbed. The electrons in the rock are no different from the electrons in our brains, and the difference is quantitative.

Heat and quantum jitter decide it, so it is only chance

The physicist is hallucinating, because heat and quantum jitter decide nothing except by using their own free will also. The real illusion is randomness. What looks like perfect randomness is a simulation of very random randomness, caused by the interweaving tapestry of nearly infinite strands of conjoined free will.

“Could have done otherwise” is just as unfalsifiable

That fails as an objection. For the proposition that you could have done otherwise there is strong evidence, which is everyone’s shared intuition that it is true. For the proposition that you could not have done otherwise there is none.

There is no self, so no one to be free

This misunderstands the nature of consciousness, which objects have too. All boundaries are illusory to a very significant degree, and so are the strands of free will.

Star Wars says there is a fate

We grant that the story says it, and that its characters claim it. We reject the premise, and think the writer would be kind to our objection.
